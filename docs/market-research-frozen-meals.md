## 2. Synthesis

**Price band (indicative only).**
- Retail premium (Woolworths): about R100-R190 per meal, depending on pack size.
- Frozen delivery boxes (Dinnerbox): maybe R30-R110 per meal [UNVERIFIED].
- Charity or institutional: R15-R55 per meal.
- Reasonable working band for Manna: R70-R120 per main of 350-450 g. Test this against live prices before fixing.

**Delivery norms.**
- Free delivery above a minimum basket is common (Forever Frozen R750).
- Flat fees of about R50-R150 are seen, and some dated.
- Same-day local delivery is rare. Courier with insulated packaging is typical.
- Redelivery fees are charged when no one is home. Plan for this with elderly customers.

**Gaps and opportunities for Manna.**
1. Almost nobody in the results speaks to seniors or carers directly. Meals on Wheels does, but it is charity-driven and branch-limited.
2. No medical or dietary positioning was found in the commercial players. Diabetic-friendly, low-salt and soft-texture options look open [UNVERIFIED for all competitors].
3. Pretoria North home cooking with a personal feel. Local, named cook, small batches.
4. Children or adult children ordering for parents: a gifting or "feed Mom" flow.
5. Weekly planning help. Offer a simple "weekly pack" for people who want no decisions.
6. WhatsApp ordering. Many seniors will not use an app or cart.
7. Delivery into the freezer or a unpacking service, and a phone call check-in. This is the real differentiator for disabled and elderly customers.

**Trust signals that matter.**
- Allergen labelling and full ingredients on every pack.
- Reheating instructions in large print: microwave and oven times, with a "piping hot all through" check.
- "Use by" date and storage instructions: keep at -18 C, defrost once, do not refreeze. Shelf life must come from your own testing or a food-safety consultant. Do not guess it.
- Food safety and R638 (2018, general hygiene for food premises and transport): a Certificate of Acceptability from the local municipality. Show it. It is non-transferable and issued per premises. A home kitchen generally needs the municipal environmental health practitioner to approve it first. [UNVERIFIED for Tshwane home-kitchen specifics: confirm with the municipality.] Sources: [ASC Food Safety R638 guide](https://ascfoodsafety.com/r638/), [Durban COA form](https://www.durban.gov.za/uploads/0000/6/2025/09/21/certificate-of-acceptability-for-food-premises.pdf).
- Labelling: R146 (labelling and advertising of foodstuffs). The citation year varies (2010 or 2012). One consultant says it is being replaced by draft R3337 with front-of-pack warnings, with no official confirmation [UNVERIFIED]. Check the current status. Source: [ASC 2026 legislation guide](https://ascfoodsafety.com/ultimate-guide-south-african-food-legislation-2026/).
- CGCSA: I did not research it. Check if their guidelines apply to a small producer.
- Do not claim "diabetic" or "heart-healthy" without a dietitian sign-off. Health claims are regulated. Say "lower salt, no added sugar" only if true and documented. [UNVERIFIED regulatory detail.]
- Halaal: only claim with certification. Otherwise state clearly what is in each dish (pork, alcohol).

| D1 | Malva pudding | SA favourite, freezes well | 1 portion | R40 |

Notes:
- Keep everything soft, moist and low in chilli. Offer "less salt" as a plain-language option rather than a medical claim.
- Pack single serves and a "2-serve" size for couples.
- Bundle: "week pack" of 5 meals + 2 desserts, priced below the sum of singles.
- Test every dish for freeze and reheat quality, including pastry sogginess, rice texture and sauce splitting, before launch.
- Decide a delivery model: a flat fee by zone, free above a minimum (for example R500-R750, test it), a fixed delivery day each week for Pretoria North and surrounds, and a WhatsApp ordering option.
